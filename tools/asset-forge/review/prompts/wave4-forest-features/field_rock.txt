Use case: production game asset generation; stylized-concept fidelity.

Create one standalone mossy woodland rock-cluster feature sprite. Generate at high resolution for later nearest-neighbor reduction to a native 64x64 PNG.

Reference images:
- Image 1 is the world visual master. Match its warm late-medieval pixel-art palette, crisp clustered shading, top-down three-quarter view, and chunky readable forms.
- Image 2 is the targeted field reference sheet. Use it to match the established stone shapes, terrain camera, and pixel scale.

Subject: one compact irregular cluster of two or three naturally joined weathered stones, with angular chipped edges, a broad grounded base, cool slate-grey and warm brown-grey planes, deep crevices, and restrained olive moss. It must read as one natural obstacle cluster, not a round pebble, boulder sphere, square block, rubble pile, cliff, wall, or floor tile.

Composition: exactly one rock cluster, centered and bottom-anchored, fully contained, asymmetric silhouette, roughly 20-50% visible-pixel coverage after reduction. Preserve clear transparent space around all sides. No ground tile, grass square, soil patch, shadow ellipse, scenery, border, text, UI, character, plant, or extra object.

Background: perfectly flat uniform solid #ff00ff magenta chroma-key, edge to edge. Do not use magenta anywhere in the rock or moss. No gradient, texture, vignette, lighting spill, halo, matte, or cast shadow on the background.

Rendering: deliberately pixel-art-like with hard-edged color clusters and no soft photographic blur. Keep chips, crevices, and moss shapes large enough to survive nearest-neighbor downsampling. High local contrast at the silhouette and between stone planes. Native target must remain clear and recognizable at 64x64.
